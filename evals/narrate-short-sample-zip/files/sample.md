# The Lighthouse Keeper's Inventory

Every morning, Mara counted what the sea had left her. Three lengths of blue rope. A child's shoe, **left foot only**, still laced. One glass float, green as an old bottle, unbroken after who knew how many miles.

She wrote each thing down in the ledger her father had kept, in the same narrow column, under the same faded heading: *Received*.

"You could stop," her brother said once, on one of his rare visits. "Nobody reads that book."

"The sea does," she said, and he laughed, because he thought she was joking.

* * *

The storm came in October, the way the old charts promised it would. For two nights the lamp turned and turned above her, and she did not sleep. On the third morning the beach was a different shape. The rocks had moved. The dunes had folded in on themselves like a letter.

And there, at the tide line, sat a wooden crate with her father's initials burned into the lid.

Mara stood over it for a long time. Then she went back up the stairs, opened the ledger, and under *Received* she wrote nothing at all. Not yet.
